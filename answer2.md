# Problem 2 - Paper Plane Challenge

Rules followed: one A4 sheet (21 cm x 29.7 cm), no tape, glue or clips, hand launch only. Goal is the longest time in the air.

## Design idea

To stay up longer the plane should glide slowly, not fly fast like a dart. So I used:
- wide wings for more lift at low speed
- extra folds at the nose so the weight is at the front
- a small V shape in the wings (dihedral) for stability
- small flaps at the back of the wings, bent up a little, so the nose does not dive

## Construction

1. Fold the paper in half lengthwise and open it. This is the center line.
2. Fold the top two corners to the center line.
3. Fold the slanted edges to the center line again.
4. Fold the nose tip down about 2 cm, then fold the nose back about 3 cm. This puts more paper at the front.
5. Fold the plane in half along the center line.
6. Fold each wing down so the wing edge is about 3 cm above the bottom of the body. Both sides must match.
7. Open the wings so they make a shallow V (about 10 to 15 degrees).
8. Bend up the last 1 cm of each wingtip a little.
9. Bend a small flap (about 1 cm x 3 cm) at the back edge of each wing slightly upward.

A4 paper of 80 gsm weighs about 5 g (area 0.0624 m2 x 80 g/m2). I chose wide wings because they give more lift at low speed, so the plane flies slowly and stays up longer. A narrow dart wing is faster and lands sooner.

## Forces

- Weight: about 5 g, so W = m x g = 0.005 x 9.8 = about 0.05 N.
- Lift: made by the wings and body. In a steady glide, lift balances the weight.
- Drag: air resistance. Wrinkles and rough folds increase it.
- Thrust: there is no engine. The throw gives the starting speed. After that the plane glides down and the small forward part of its weight keeps it moving.

Wing loading = weight / wing area. For example, if the area is 120 cm2 then 5 g / 120 cm2 = about 0.042 g/cm2. Lower wing loading gives a slower glide.

## Center of gravity and stability

The CG is the balance point. I find it by balancing the plane on my finger. It should be in front of the center of lift, roughly a quarter of the wing chord from the wing's front edge.
- CG too far back: the plane stalls and goes up and down.
- CG too far forward: the plane dives.
The V shape of the wings keeps the plane from rolling over, and the center fold keeps it flying straight.

## Two modifications

1. Move the nose weight back by about 1 cm (fewer nose folds). Test: 5 flights of the old design and 5 of the new one, same launch and same room, then compare the means. If the plane was diving, this should help.
2. Make the back flaps about 2 mm bigger. Test: same method. A bigger flap gives more pitch-up force, which may keep the nose level and make the glide longer.

I change only one thing at a time so I know what caused the difference.
